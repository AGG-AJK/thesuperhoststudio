What's quietly costing you stars

The same issues show up in almost every underperforming listing. Here's what I look for first.

1
Your listing doesn't answer the real question

Guests aren't reading your listing — they're scanning for answers to their specific worry. Is it actually private? Will the host respond if something goes wrong? If your copy doesn't address those directly, they'll book somewhere that does.

2
Communication gaps before check-in

Most host-guest friction happens in the 48 hours before arrival. What you send, when you send it, and how you frame expectations in that window shapes the entire stay — including the review.

3
The amenities guests mention are missing

Guests notice the charger by the bed. They remember the local guide. They write about the extra blanket in January. These aren't luxuries — they're the difference between a four-star and a five-star review.

4
No cushion for the one bad stay

Every host eventually gets a guest they can't win. The hosts who survive it are the ones with enough five-star reviews in the bank that one anomaly doesn't move their average — and who know exactly how to respond publicly.
